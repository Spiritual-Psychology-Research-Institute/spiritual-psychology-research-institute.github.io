## Title

**Recogimiento as a Path beyond the Believer's Divided Self: The Limits of
Jungian Individuation and the Restoration of Zōē's Reign**

## Abstract

Contemporary Christians frequently report a gap between what they experience in
worship and how they live the rest of the week. This study names that gap the
believer's divided self and argues that it reflects a deeper condition: the
confinement of the human spirit beneath the soulish ego (*psychikos*) and bodily
instinct, so that spiritual awakening remains episodic rather than governing.

The division is first read through Carl Jung's analytical psychology,
particularly the persona–shadow dynamic, which usefully dismantles the ego's
claim to self-transparency and opens the unconscious to reflection.
Individuation, however, cannot carry the believer to whole-person
sanctification. Because the ego–Self axis remains intrapsychic, the Self it
reaches is an immanent center of wholeness rather than the personal Christ who
indwells; integration is achieved, but the transfer of sovereignty is not.

Working from a tripartite anthropology of spirit, soul and body, the study
proposes *recogimiento* — the prayer of recollection as developed by Francisco
de Osuna and deepened by Teresa of Ávila — as a spiritual-theological
alternative: a whole-person practice that gathers the soul's dispersed faculties
inward toward the indwelling Christ. Through a self-reflective case study, it
traces how acquired recollection (*recogimiento adquirido*), sustained by the
one praying, gives way to infused recollection (*recogimiento infuso*), in which
God takes the initiative, and how that transition enacts a reversal of
sovereignty in which the indwelling Christ lays hold of the whole person. The
study concludes that as the resources surfaced by psychological insight are
progressively surrendered to Christ's rule, the believer's fragmented life is
gathered into the reign of *zōē* — the divine life that governs, not merely the
life one lives.

## Keywords

*recogimiento* (prayer of recollection), Teresa of Ávila, individuation,
spiritual theology, tripartite anthropology, *zōē*
